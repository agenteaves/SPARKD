Verified Actions. Transparent Process. Meaningful Impact.

1. Introduction

Proof of Deeds is a protocol designed to create a transparent, evidence-based process for reviewing and recording real-world acts of positive impact. Rather than measuring popularity or personal recognition, the protocol focuses on whether sufficient evidence supports a claimed action. The objective is to strengthen trust through clear standards, documented review procedures, and respect for participant privacy.

2. Vision

Our vision is to build a trusted system where positive actions can be documented, reviewed, and preserved with transparency. Proof of Deeds seeks to encourage meaningful community impact while reducing opportunities for fraud, manipulation, and misinformation.

3. Core Principles

  • Truth Over Popularity
  • Privacy by Default
  • Transparency
  • Accountability
  • Fairness
  • Continuous Improvement

4. Roles

Submitter

Performs a deed and submits supporting evidence.

Reviewer

Reviews evidence according to protocol standards.

Moderator

Handles disputes, abuse reports, and policy enforcement.

Observer

Views public records and community impact.

6. Evidence Standards

Evidence requirements depend on the type and scale of the claimed action. Examples of supporting evidence may include receipts, organization confirmations, event records, witness statements, photographs, or other relevant documentation. Evidence should support the claim while minimizing unnecessary disclosure of personal information.

7. Safety Standards

  • No nudity or sexual content
  • No graphic violence
  • No hate or harassment
  • No illegal content
  • No malware or harmful files
  • No unrelated submissions
  • Protect participant privacy whenever possible

8. Reputation System

Reviewers build trust through a consistent history of accurate, fair, and protocol-compliant reviews. A reviewer's reputation reflects their participation history and helps strengthen confidence in the review process. Reputation should support careful decision-making rather than encourage competition.

9. Appeals

When a submission is rejected, the submitter may provide additional evidence and request another review. Appeals are intended to improve fairness while maintaining the integrity of the verification process.
